About the Project

01

What Is the Harmony Letters Project?

The Harmony Letters Project is a passion project created by three students who share a love for music. Developed within four days as part of our Web Development course, this website showcases our understanding of web development while bringing our shared passion to life.

Our idea is simple: to share the music we love with others, express our thoughts on our favourite songs, discover new music, and exchange opinions with fellow music lovers. We believe that every song has a story to tell, and every listener has a different perspective worth sharing.

02

The Meaning Behind the Music

Music has been a part of humanity since the very beginning — from the rhythm of footsteps and the sound of laughter to a mother's gentle humming as she lulls her baby to sleep.

It is a wordless language, a way of expressing our feelings and emotions without having to say a single word. For those who struggle to put their feelings into words, or those still learning to understand their emotions, music can speak where words fall short.

Sometimes, a song says everything we cannot. And sometimes, hearing a song that resonates with us reminds us that we are not alone in what we feel.

03

How It Works

Getting started is simple. Create an account, search for any song you like and share your thoughts about it. Click publish and let others discover your perspective.

You can explore the thoughts and opinions of fellow music lovers, discover songs you may never have heard before, and join a community built around a shared appreciation for music.

04

Our Purpose

There is something special about connecting with people who share the same love for music. A song that means the world to you might hold a completely different meaning for someone else, and that is what makes sharing music so meaningful.

Through the Harmony Letters Project, we hope to create a space where people can express themselves, discover different perspectives, and connect with others through the songs they love. After all, music is not just something we listen to — it is something we experience, feel, and share.
